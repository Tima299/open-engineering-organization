# Copy rules

## Diagnose before editing
| Input | Allowed change |
|---|---|
| Blank page | Write from `.oeo/product-marketing.md`; reuse customer phrases verbatim where they fit |
| Existing copy with a real voice | Keep the voice; fix clarity, claims and structure only |
| Point edit request | Change only what was asked; flag other issues without fixing them |

## Line gate
1. **Picturable**: a reader can imagine the concrete situation or result.
2. **Falsifiable**: it could be proven wrong (numbers, named outcomes, specific behavior).
3. **Ownable**: a competitor could not paste it onto their own page unchanged.
4. **Supported**: every claim maps to a proof point in the context file. Otherwise remove it, or
   state the evidence that would unlock it.

## Patterns that read as machine-written (rewrite them)
- Contrast reveals ("It's not X. It's Y.") and stacked negation lists ("No A. No B. No C.").
- A trailing pile-on clause after a complete sentence (", making it faster, simpler and safer").
- Stock openers ("In today's fast-paced world", "Unlock", "Supercharge", "Seamlessly").
- Triplets used for rhythm rather than meaning; vague intensifiers (very, truly, incredibly).
- Em dashes in short-form copy (headlines, buttons, social posts).
- Invented social proof, metrics or testimonials. These are never allowed, not even as placeholders.

## Formats
- Headline: the outcome for the reader, at most 10 words. Subhead: how, plus the proof point.
- Button: verb + object ("Install the plugin"), never "Learn more" alone.
- Release announcement: what changed for the user → who it is for → how to try it (one command)
  → limits and known issues → link to the changelog.
